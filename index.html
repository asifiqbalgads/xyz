Google Ads Case Study

Reducing Cost Per Lead by 37% for a Toronto Electrical Contractor

Industry: Electrical Renovation Market: Toronto, Canada Platform: Google Ads (Search) Duration: Nov 2025 – Feb 2026 Currency: SGD (Singapore Dollar)

Background

The client is an electrical renovation contractor based in Toronto. They had been running Google Ads but were not getting enough leads for what they were spending. The account had no clear structure — too many irrelevant clicks, no negative keyword list, and a landing page that wasn't converting well.

I took over management in November 2025 and ran the account for four months.

The first two months were about understanding the account and gathering real data before making changes.

What was wrong

  • Keywords were too broad — pulling in unrelated searches
  • No negative keyword list — budget was leaking on irrelevant clicks
  • Bidding strategy was not optimized for conversions yet
  • Landing page had no clear contact form or phone number visible at the top
  • Conversion rate of 5.06% was low for a local service campaign in this niche

Phase 2 — Jan & Feb 2026

After analyzing two months of search term data, I made targeted changes to fix the issues identified in Phase 1.

What I changed

  • Switched from broad match to phrase and exact match keywords
  • Built a negative keyword list — removed searches like "DIY electrical," "free quote," "training," and "jobs"
  • Moved bidding toward a conversion-focused strategy once enough data existed
  • Recommended landing page edits — moved the phone number and form above the fold
  • Cut campaigns that were spending without converting

Mistakes I Made & What I Learned

❌ My Mistakes (Phase 1)
  • Waited too long before adding negative keywords
  • Kept broad match keywords running for too long
  • Flagged the landing page issue late — should have been done before launch
  • Did not review search term reports weekly in the first month
✅ What I'd Do Differently
  • Build a negative keyword list on day one based on the niche
  • Start with phrase and exact match, not broad
  • Run a landing page audit before the campaign goes live
  • Review search terms every week, not bi-weekly

Key Takeaways

  • Fewer clicks can mean better results — quality of traffic matters more than volume
  • Negative keywords had the biggest single impact on cost per lead
  • The conversion rate nearly doubled — not from more budget, but from smarter targeting
  • Phase 1 data was not wasted — it told us exactly where the money was leaking
  • For local service campaigns, match type control is non-negotiable from the start
